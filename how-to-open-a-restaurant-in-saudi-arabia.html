How to open a restaurant in Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia’s restaurant scene has changed faster than almost any other sector: new districts, a delivery economy that never sleeps, and diners who expect a concept, not just a menu. The licensing route is digital and well defined. The real risk sits elsewhere, in choosing the wrong format, signing for a site that cannot carry a commercial kitchen, and launching a menu that breaks the SFDA disclosure rules on day one.

Information as of 12 October 2026 · Checked against official Saudi sources · About 15 min read

At a glance

  • Pick the format first: dine-in, delivery-only cloud kitchen or catering each changes your site, fit-out, staffing and licence profile.
  • Licensing stack: MISA if any owner is foreign, then a CR with the right food activities, a Balady commercial licence issued with the Civil Defense safety permit, and Balady health certificates for every food handler.
  • Kitchen and site drive the timeline: Balady needs a registered lease or deed, the building permit and a shopfront photo, and may inspect before licensing.
  • Menus and delivery apps: since 1 July 2025, menus and ordering platforms must flag high-salt items, show caffeine in drinks and show the activity time needed to burn the calories.
  • After opening: CR annual confirmation, VAT from SAR 375,000 of annual supplies, e-invoicing, Qiwa and GOSI.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for founders opening a food business with a kitchen: a dine-in restaurant, a quick-service outlet, a delivery-only cloud kitchen, or a catering company serving events and offices. It covers the decisions, the licences and how they connect, the order to do things in, and what you must keep doing once the doors are open. If you are opening a coffee-led concept, our café guide is the better starting point; if you want to test a concept before committing to a lease, look at a food truck first.

Choose your format: dine-in, cloud kitchen or catering

The format you choose decides more about your business than the cuisine does. Each has a different cost structure and a different licensing profile.

FormatWhere the money goesWhat to watch
Dine-in restaurantPrime rent, front-of-house fit-out, larger teamFootfall versus rent; customer-facing safety and accessibility; longest fit-out
Cloud kitchen (delivery only)Kitchen equipment, platform commissions, marketingPlatform dependency; every menu on every app must meet the SFDA disclosure rules
CateringProduction kitchen, transport, event staffFood safety in transport and holding; contracts and deposits; seasonal demand

Many operators combine formats, for example a dine-in flagship that also runs delivery and corporate catering from the same kitchen. That is efficient, but register each activity you will genuinely run on your commercial registration and check its municipal requirements, because an activity you trade under without registering is a compliance problem waiting to surface.

Ownership, legal form and budget

Ownership. Saudi and GCC-owned restaurants go straight to the Saudi Business Center. Any foreign ownership means registering the investment with MISA first; MISA reviews a complete file within about ten days, and the CR follows its registration notice. See our MISA registration guide.

Legal form. A restaurant carries real liabilities: leases, supplier credit, employees and food safety risk. That is why most founders prefer a limited liability company or a simplified joint-stock company over trading as a sole establishment. A partnership with an experienced operator or chef often works best inside an LLC with clear articles on decision-making and exits. Our business structures guide sets out the trade-offs.

Budget. No official source sets the cost of opening a restaurant, and published estimates vary so much that they are not useful. Build your budget from the bottom up: rent and deposits, kitchen engineering and extraction, equipment, front-of-house fit-out, licensing and certificate fees, pre-opening wages and training, opening stock, launch marketing and enough working capital to carry the business to break-even. Our Saudi café and restaurant plan model is designed for exactly this exercise.

Step by step

Step 1: Read the municipal requirements for your activities

Use Balady’s free inquiry service to look up each activity by name or ISIC code and print its municipal requirements. Do this for every format you plan to run. Give the printouts to your kitchen designer and fit-out contractor before they draw anything.

Step 2: MISA (foreign owners only)

Register the investment and wait for the registration notice before issuing the CR.

Step 3: Commercial registration

Apply through the Saudi Business Center. The registrar decides a complete application within ten days. One CR can carry several activities, so a restaurant that also caters can hold both. Our CR guide explains the application and what follows.

Step 4: Secure a site that can carry a commercial kitchen

Balady asks for a lease, title deed or investment contract, a copy of the building permit, an exterior photo showing the sign and, depending on the contract and activity, a sanitation contract. For instant-licence activities it also needs a Civil Defense safety equipment invoice or safety report. For a restaurant, also check the practical points that make or break a kitchen: extraction routes, drainage, gas or electrical capacity, waste storage and delivery access. A beautiful unit without a viable extraction route is not a restaurant site.

Step 5: Design and fit-out

Design the kitchen around a clean workflow, from goods-in to cold storage, preparation, cooking, holding, service and washing, without crossing raw and ready-to-eat flows. Size your refrigeration for peak delivery days, not average days. Keep a full set of drawings and equipment specifications, because you will need them for the licence, for inspections and for any later change.

Step 6: Balady commercial licence and Civil Defense permit

Enter your establishment record, activity and area, locate the unit and complete the shop details. Instant-licence activities pay and receive the licence; others go to the municipality for review and possibly an inspection. Balady quotes one to ten days. The safety permit is issued together with the licence.

Step 7: Health certificates for the whole kitchen and service team

Each food handler needs a Balady health certificate: a request on Balady, verification of the licence and the worker’s profession, a medical examination at a Ministry of Health-approved centre, and the health education programme where the city requires it. Allow about ten days per worker, and line up examinations as soon as offers are accepted.

Step 8: ZATCA, Qiwa and GOSI

Register for tax, open your Qiwa establishment file and register employees with GOSI. Recruiting chefs from abroad takes time; start the work permit and visa process early and plan opening around it.

Step 9: Menu engineering, delivery apps and soft opening

Engineer the menu for margin and compliance at the same time (see below), load it consistently onto your point of sale and every delivery platform, and run a soft opening with real covers to test ticket times.

Menu disclosure and food safety

From 1 July 2025, SFDA rules require food establishments to display detailed nutrition information on all menus, physical and online, including on food ordering platforms:

  • A salt icon next to any item with more than 5 grams of salt (2,000 mg sodium).
  • The caffeine content of beverages, in milligrams per 100 ml or per cup, with a statement that adults should not exceed 400 mg a day.
  • The estimated physical activity time needed to burn the calories in each item, shown with icons and a duration.

For a restaurant, the salt rule is the one that tends to surprise kitchens. Sauces, marinades, stocks and bread add up quickly, and a generous portion can cross the threshold on its own. Standardise recipes with weighed ingredients, calculate nutrition per portion, and treat any recipe change as a menu change. If you run delivery, remember that each platform’s listing is a menu for these purposes.

Food safety itself is an everyday discipline: supplier approval, receiving temperatures, cold and hot holding, cooling of cooked food, labelling and date control, allergen management, pest control and cleaning records. Catering adds transport and holding at events, which is where most temperature failures happen.

If you add catering or online ordering

Catering contracts are usually agreed in advance with deposits, so put clear terms in writing: menu, numbers, timings, cancellation and what happens if guest numbers change. If customers order and pay online through your own website or app, you are also a service provider under the E-Commerce Law, which means disclosing your identity, contact details and CR on the store, giving clear terms and total prices including taxes and delivery, and issuing an itemised invoice. The law’s general seven-day right to cancel does not apply to catering services. Our online business guide covers the e-commerce rules in more depth.

Realistic timeline

The government steps are short on paper: a CR decision within ten days, a Balady licence in one to ten days, health certificates in about ten days and MISA registration in up to about ten days for foreign owners. The real drivers are finding a site that can carry a kitchen, design approvals, fit-out, equipment lead times and recruitment. Run the paperwork in parallel with the fit-out, and do not commit to an opening date until the extraction and equipment installation dates are confirmed.

What you owe once you are trading

  • Commercial register. Confirm your data every year and update changes within 15 days. A confirmation 90 days late suspends the CR and every licence issued under it.
  • Licence and certificates. Keep the Balady licence valid and every food handler’s health certificate current.
  • VAT and e-invoicing. Register once annual taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 (voluntary from SAR 187,500), file returns on time and issue compliant e-invoices. See our VAT guide.
  • People. Keep Qiwa and GOSI up to date and watch your Saudization position as the team grows.
  • Menus. Update disclosures everywhere whenever a recipe or portion changes.

Building the team

A restaurant is a people business before it is a food business. Plan the team around your service model: how many covers or orders you expect at peak, how many stations the kitchen runs, and who owns opening, closing, stock and cash. Write job descriptions before you recruit, because Qiwa contracts, work permits and health certificates all depend on the profession you register for each person.

  • Leadership. A head chef who owns recipes, food cost and food safety, and a manager who owns service, rotas and cash. In a small operation the founder often holds one of these roles. Be honest about which.
  • Kitchen and service. Size the team for peak trading, then use rotas to manage quieter periods rather than hiring for the average.
  • Saudization. Requirements are set by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development and tracked in Qiwa. Check your establishment’s position before each hire, not after.
  • Training. Every food handler needs a health certificate, and some cities require the health education programme. Build food safety, allergen handling and menu disclosure into induction so that staff can answer guests’ questions accurately.

Working with delivery platforms

For many Saudi restaurants, delivery is now a large share of revenue, and for cloud kitchens it is all of it. Treat each platform as a separate sales channel with its own economics. Commission, promotions and packaging can turn a profitable dine-in dish into a loss-maker on delivery, so price delivery menus separately and design dishes that travel well. Keep one master menu with recipes, prices and nutrition data, and push changes to every platform from that single source; inconsistent listings are the most common way restaurants fall out of line with the SFDA disclosure rules. Finally, read each platform’s contract for exclusivity, payment terms and who bears the cost of refunds, before you sign rather than after the first dispute.

Scenarios

A family-style restaurant in a new residential district. Two Saudi partners form an LLC with clear articles on who runs the kitchen and who runs the business. They reject their first-choice unit because there is no viable extraction route, and choose a corner unit with service access instead. The licence comes through quickly; recruitment of experienced cooks sets the opening date.

A delivery-only burger brand. The founder opens a cloud kitchen in a light commercial unit, registers the food activities on the CR and licenses the kitchen through Balady. The most work goes into menu compliance: each burger and sauce combination is weighed and calculated so the salt icons and calorie-burn times are correct on four delivery platforms.

A corporate catering company. A chef with a hotel background sets up a production kitchen, licenses it, and wins office lunch contracts. Insulated transport and temperature logs at drop-off become part of every delivery, and contracts set out cancellation terms clearly because catering is excluded from the statutory seven-day cancellation right for online orders.

Common mistakes

  • Signing a lease before confirming the building permit, lease registration and kitchen services.
  • Running a format, such as catering, that is not registered as an activity.
  • Launching on delivery apps with menus that lack salt, caffeine or calorie-burn information.
  • Hiring kitchen staff without allowing time for health certificates and work permits.
  • Forgetting the CR annual confirmation and losing the licence with it.
  • Under-budgeting working capital for the months before break-even.

Frequently asked questions

What licences does a restaurant need in Saudi Arabia?

A commercial registration with the right food activities, a Balady commercial licence for the premises issued together with the Civil Defense safety permit, health certificates for food handlers, and ZATCA, Qiwa and GOSI registrations. Foreign-owned restaurants register with MISA first.

Can I run a cloud kitchen in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. A delivery-only kitchen still needs a CR, a Balady licence for the premises and health certificates for staff, and every delivery platform listing must meet the SFDA menu disclosure rules.

Do I need a separate licence for catering?

Register catering as an activity on your CR if you will offer it, and check its municipal requirements through Balady’s activity inquiry service. One CR can carry several activities.

What must a Saudi restaurant menu show?

Since 1 July 2025: a salt icon on items above 5 g salt (2,000 mg sodium), caffeine content for beverages, and the activity time needed to burn each item’s calories, on physical and online menus and ordering platforms.

Can customers cancel catering orders placed online within seven days?

The E-Commerce Law’s general seven-day cancellation right does not apply to catering services, so your own contract terms on cancellation matter. Set them out clearly before taking deposits.

How long does it take to open a restaurant?

Government steps are short (CR within 10 days, Balady licence in 1 to 10 days, health certificates in about 10 days). Site selection, kitchen fit-out, equipment and recruitment usually take far longer.
