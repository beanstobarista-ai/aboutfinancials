How to start an online business in Saudi Arabia

Launching a store in Saudi Arabia takes an afternoon on any store builder. Launching one that customers trust, payment providers approve and the Ministry of Commerce considers compliant takes a little more thought. The rules are not heavy, but they are specific: who you are on paper, which bank account the money lands in, what your store must disclose and what you owe customers when an order goes wrong.

Information as of 12 October 2026 · Checked against official Saudi sources · About 14 min read

At a glance

  • Your legal base: a commercial registration (any business, including foreign-owned ones after MISA) or, for Saudi individuals in listed professions, an HRSD freelance document.
  • Store authentication: done only on the Saudi Business Center’s Business Platform (which replaced Maroof in 2023), with a CR or valid freelance document and a commercial bank account linked to the store.
  • E-Commerce Law duties: disclose who you are and your CR, show total prices including taxes and delivery, issue an itemised invoice, honour the seven-day cancellation right where it applies, and deliver within 15 days unless agreed otherwise.
  • Penalties under the law reach fines of up to SAR 1,000,000, suspension of the activity or blocking of the store.
  • VAT is mandatory above SAR 375,000 of annual taxable supplies.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for founders selling to Saudi consumers online: a clothing or abaya brand on a store builder, a perfume or beauty label, a dropshipping store, a seller of digital products, or a service business that takes bookings and payments online. It also matters to restaurants and home businesses that take orders through their own website. It sets out the decisions, the registrations, the rules your store must follow and the operating habits that keep an online business out of trouble.

Dropshipping deserves a special note. It is attractive because it needs little capital, but the E-Commerce Law treats you as the service provider. If your supplier takes three weeks to ship, it is your customer who can rescind and claim a refund, and your store that faces the complaint. Choose suppliers on delivery reliability before price.

Your legal base: CR or freelance document

The E-Commerce Law recognises two kinds of online seller. A merchant is registered in the Commercial Register; a practitioner practises e-commerce without being registered. Both are service providers with the same core duties to consumers. In practice, though, the authentication and banking system pushes every serious store towards a formal base.

Commercial registration

A CR is the default for any business that plans to hire, hold stock, raise money or scale. The law also requires a merchant to register their e-shop in the Commercial Register. Under the 2024 Commercial Register Law, applications are electronic, the registrar decides within ten days, and one CR can hold several activities. Our CR guide covers the process and the annual confirmation that follows, and our business structures guide helps you choose between an establishment, an LLC and a simplified joint-stock company.

Freelance document

For a Saudi individual whose activity is on the freelance platform’s list, HRSD’s freelance document is a lighter alternative. Saudi citizens aged 18 to 60 can apply through the platform using Nafath; the document is valid for one year and renewable, and the Ministry of Commerce accepts a valid freelance document as the basis for store authentication. Our home business guide explains the application step by step.

Foreign founders

A foreign investor must register with MISA before setting up. Pure trading is a demanding category for foreign ownership: MISA’s Investor Guide sets a minimum capital of SAR 30,000,000 for 100% foreign-owned commercial (trading) activity, together with a presence in at least three regional or international markets. Many foreign founders therefore partner with a Saudi company or choose a different activity. Read our MISA guide before you plan around a structure. Note also that the E-Commerce Law applies to sellers outside the Kingdom who offer products or services to consumers in the Kingdom in a way that gives those consumers access.

Store authentication on the Business Platform

In March 2023 the Ministry of Commerce asked online stores to register on the Business Platform, operated by the Saudi Business Center, instead of Maroof, and it describes the Business Platform as the only approved channel for verifying online stores in the Kingdom. The requirements are:

  • An active commercial registration or a valid freelance document.
  • A commercial bank account linked to the store. Personal accounts are not accepted, and the ministry warns consumers against transferring money to personal accounts.

Authenticated stores are tied to that linked commercial account and can be looked up in the ministry’s verified store inquiry service. Since September 2023, the Commercial Complaint app and the 1900 reporting centre act on reports about authenticated stores. Display your authentication clearly on your store; most store builders have a field for it.

What the E-Commerce Law requires of your store

Disclosures on the store

  • Your name or identifying details and your address, unless you are registered with an authentication entity.
  • Your contact information.
  • The name and number of your commercial registration, if you have one.
  • If you practise a licensed profession: the body you are registered with, your licence details and your professional title.

Before and after the sale

  • Terms and conditions, the steps to conclude the contract, the product’s main characteristics, the total price including all charges, taxes and delivery fees, payment and delivery arrangements, and any warranty.
  • An itemised invoice after the sale, showing the total price, charges, taxes and delivery fees, plus the delivery date and place.

Advertising

An online advertisement is part of the contract and is legally binding. It must name the product and the seller with contact details, and it must not contain false or misleading claims or use a counterfeit logo or a trademark you have no right to use. The ministry can order a non-compliant ad to be corrected or withdrawn within one day of notification. That matters for influencer campaigns: the claims in the post bind you.

Cancellation and delivery

  • Seven days to cancel. A consumer may rescind within seven days of receiving the product, or of the service contract, provided the product has not been used or the service received. The consumer bears the cost of rescinding unless you agree otherwise.
  • Exceptions include customised or made-to-order products (unless defective or not as agreed), used videos, records, discs or software, newspapers, magazines and books, damage caused by the consumer’s mishandling, accommodation, transport and catering services, and downloadable software unless it is faulty.
  • Late delivery. If you delay delivery by more than 15 days from the contract or the agreed date, the consumer can rescind and claim a refund plus costs caused by the delay, unless the delay is due to force majeure. You must notify customers of anticipated delays.

Customer data

Keep customer data only as long as the transaction requires unless a different period is agreed, protect its confidentiality, and do not use it for unauthorised purposes or share it without consent or a legal requirement. You are responsible for data held by your agents and service providers too.

Penalties

Violations can lead to a warning, a fine of up to SAR 1,000,000, temporary or permanent suspension of the activity, or partial or complete blocking of the store. In urgent cases the minister can block a store pending a committee decision. Penalty decisions can be published at the violator’s expense.

Step by step

  1. Choose the model and products, and check whether any product category you sell needs its own approval from a sector regulator such as the SFDA.
  2. Get your legal base: CR (with MISA first if any owner is foreign) or a freelance document.
  3. Open a commercial bank account in the business’s or document holder’s name.
  4. Build the store with the required disclosures, clear terms, total pricing, a returns and cancellation policy that reflects the law, and a privacy policy.
  5. Authenticate the store on the Business Platform and display the authentication.
  6. Set up payments and invoicing. Once VAT-registered, invoices must meet ZATCA’s e-invoicing rules.
  7. Agree delivery standards with couriers and suppliers that keep you well inside the 15-day limit.
  8. Launch, then measure: conversion, returns, delivery times and complaints.

Tax and accounts

VAT registration is mandatory once annual taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 and optional from SAR 187,500. Online businesses can cross the threshold suddenly, after a big campaign or a seasonal peak, so test it monthly on a rolling twelve-month basis. Once registered, your displayed prices must include VAT, because the law requires the total price including taxes to be shown. Our VAT guide covers the monthly test, registration and filing. If you trade under a CR, remember the annual CR confirmation too.

Operations that make or break an online store

Fulfilment and delivery

Choose couriers on reliability and coverage, not just price, and set delivery promises you can keep across the regions you sell to. Tell customers the expected delivery date at checkout and update them if anything changes; the law requires you to notify customers of anticipated delays, and proactive messages prevent most complaints.

Returns and refunds

Write one clear returns policy that reflects the seven-day rule and its exceptions, explains who pays for return shipping, and states how quickly refunds are processed. Make it visible on product pages and at checkout, not hidden in the footer. Track return reasons; they tell you which products, sizes or descriptions need fixing.

Payments

Use a payment provider that settles into your commercial account. Offer the payment methods Saudi customers expect, and reconcile payouts to orders weekly so that missing settlements and chargebacks are caught early.

Product pages

Because advertisements and product descriptions form part of the contract, write them from verified specifications. Show the total price, including VAT once registered and any delivery fee, before the customer commits.

Marketing without compliance risk

Influencer and social campaigns drive most Saudi online sales, and everything said in them counts as an advertisement. Brief creators in writing: approved claims only, the correct product name, your store name and contact details, and no use of logos or trademarks you do not own. Keep a copy of every approved post. If the ministry asks for an ad to be corrected or withdrawn, you may have only one day to act, so know who can take a post down at short notice.

Choosing a store platform and payment set-up

Most Saudi founders start on a hosted store builder rather than a custom site, and that is usually the right call. When comparing platforms, look beyond the monthly fee: Arabic and English storefronts, local payment methods, integrations with Saudi couriers, a field to display your Business Platform authentication, ZATCA-compliant invoicing once you register for VAT, and how easily you can export your customer and order data if you move later. Register your domain and social handles in the business’s name, not a developer’s or a friend’s.

Keep the store’s legal pages under your control: terms and conditions, the returns and cancellation policy, the privacy policy and the disclosure block with your name, contact details and CR or freelance document. Review them whenever you add a new product category, because cancellation exceptions and licence disclosures can change with what you sell.

Scenarios

An abaya brand on a store builder. The founder registers a single-person LLC, opens a business account, authenticates the store and displays the certificate in the footer. Because tailored abayas are made to measure, her policy separates ready-to-wear items, which carry the seven-day right, from custom orders, which are an exception unless defective.

A dropshipping store selling home gadgets. The owner tests suppliers on delivery times first, drops two that cannot deliver within two weeks, and writes product descriptions only from verified specifications, because advertisements bind the seller.

A freelance fitness coach selling online programmes. He issues a freelance document under his profession, links a commercial account and authenticates his store. Because his programmes include downloadable content, he checks the cancellation exceptions before writing his refund policy.

Common mistakes

  • Taking payments into a personal account.
  • Displaying prices without VAT or delivery fees.
  • Copying refund policies from foreign stores that ignore the seven-day rule or its exceptions.
  • Letting supplier delays run past 15 days without telling customers.
  • Influencer posts with claims you cannot support.
  • Forgetting the CR annual confirmation or the freelance document renewal.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a commercial registration to sell online in Saudi Arabia?

Not always. Store authentication on the Business Platform accepts either a CR or a valid HRSD freelance document, plus a commercial bank account linked to the store. Businesses that plan to grow, hire or hold stock usually choose a CR.

Is Maroof still used to verify online stores?

No. In 2023 the Ministry of Commerce moved store verification to the Saudi Business Center’s Business Platform and describes it as the only approved channel.

Is dropshipping legal in Saudi Arabia?

Selling online through suppliers who ship directly is not prohibited, but you are the service provider under the E-Commerce Law, so disclosure, pricing, delivery-time and cancellation duties fall on you.

How long do customers have to return an online purchase?

Seven days from receiving the product, if it has not been used, with exceptions such as customised products, used software and media, publications, catering, accommodation and transport services.

What happens if delivery is late?

If delivery is more than 15 days late from the contract or agreed date, the customer can rescind and claim a refund and related costs, unless the delay is due to force majeure or a later date was agreed.

Can a foreigner open an online store in Saudi Arabia?

Foreign investors must register with MISA. For 100% foreign-owned trading activity, MISA’s Investor Guide sets a minimum capital of SAR 30,000,000 and presence in at least three regional or international markets.

Do I have to show my CR number on my online store?

Yes, if you have one. The E-Commerce Law requires the store to disclose the name and number of the commercial registration, along with contact information and identifying details unless registered with an authentication entity.

What are the penalties for breaking the E-Commerce Law?

A warning, a fine of up to SAR 1,000,000, temporary or permanent suspension of the e-commerce activity, or partial or complete blocking of the store. Decisions can be published at the violator’s expense.
