How to start a food truck business in Saudi Arabia

A food truck is the lowest-risk way into Saudi food service. It lets you test a concept, build a following and learn your numbers before you sign a ten-year lease. The licensing route for Saudi individuals is unusually light, with no commercial registration needed. The operating rules, rewritten by the Ministry of Municipalities and Housing in 2025, are where the real discipline lies.

Information as of 12 October 2026 · Checked against official Saudi sources · About 13 min read

At a glance

  • Two routes: a Saudi individual can get a Balady mobile cart licence without a commercial registration; a company with a CR licenses carts through Balady’s commercial licence service.
  • Individual eligibility: Saudi national, 18 or over, not a government employee, and not already holding a mobile cart, retail outlet or commercial licence.
  • Freelance certificate included: once the mobile cart licence is issued, a freelance work certificate is issued automatically.
  • Where you can trade: designated sites only, such as municipal, government, private-establishment or investment sites and municipal incubators, never in residential neighbourhoods and at least 10 metres from fuel stations.
  • On board: potable water, no sauce preparation inside the unit, interior temperature not above 25°C, allergen and meat-source information on display, electronic payments and the unified electronic code.

Why a food truck, and who it suits

The economics of a truck are simple: lower fixed costs than a shop, the ability to follow demand, and a direct line to customers who will tell you very quickly whether your product works. It suits a founder who wants to prove a concept, a chef building a personal brand, a coffee specialist who wants to go where the crowds are, and an established restaurant that wants a mobile presence at events.

It is less suited to concepts that need extensive preparation on site. The 2025 municipal requirements prohibit preparing sauces inside the unit, and space for cold storage and washing is limited, so the best truck menus are short and built around a central production kitchen or careful prep. If your concept needs a full kitchen, compare this route with our restaurant guide.

The individual route in detail

Balady’s mobile cart licence lets an individual start a business without a commercial registration and without visiting the municipality. The conditions are that you are a Saudi national, at least 18, not a government employee, not on the municipal suspension list, and do not already hold a mobile cart licence, a retail outlet licence or a commercial licence. The service needs no documents: you access it, fill in the licence details, pay the fees and submit. Balady quotes one to ten days. Once issued, a freelance work certificate is generated for you automatically, which matters for banking, payment acceptance and access to programmes aimed at self-employed Saudis.

Note the restriction on holding another commercial licence. If you already run a shop or a licensed establishment, the individual route is not for you; license the truck through your business instead.

The business route

If you want a fleet, investors or a fixed production kitchen alongside the truck, set up a business first. Saudi and GCC owners register through the Saudi Business Center; foreign owners register with MISA first. Once the CR is issued, the Balady commercial licence service lets you choose the location and fill in the details of a shop or a mobile cart. Our CR guide and business structures guide cover that set-up.

The 2025 mobile unit requirements

In 2025 the Ministry of Municipalities and Housing approved new municipal requirements for mobile units, announced in November 2025 with the full text published in Arabic. The headline points every operator should design around:

Location

  • Units operate only from designated locations: sites belonging to municipalities, government entities, private establishments, investment zones or municipal incubators.
  • They must stay at least 10 metres from fuel stations.
  • They may not operate inside residential neighbourhoods, near emergency exits or near sources of environmental pollution.
  • Operation requires both the licence and a site permit from the relevant authority, and units must keep to designated working hours.

The vehicle

  • Equipment that is non-slip and easy to clean.
  • Ventilation and air conditioning that prevent vapour condensation and keep the interior at no more than 25°C.
  • Thermal insulation, appropriate lighting and safety measures, plus a first aid kit on board.
  • No loudspeakers and no smoking inside the unit.

Food safety and customer information

  • Potable water only, and dedicated equipment for preparing and washing fruit and vegetables.
  • No sauce preparation inside the unit.
  • Cooling, heating and thawing to the SFDA’s standards.
  • Clear display of product names, ingredients, allergens, temperatures and expiry dates, and a visible sign stating the type and source of any meat used.
  • Electronic payment must always be available, and the unified electronic code must be prominently displayed on the unit.

Read the full Arabic text before you buy or convert a vehicle. It is far cheaper to specify a truck correctly than to retrofit air conditioning, insulation or water systems after an inspection.

Step by step

Step 1: Define a short, prep-friendly menu

Design four to eight items that can be finished quickly on board from prepared components. Sauces, under the 2025 requirements, must come ready-made rather than being prepared in the unit. Decide where preparation happens and make sure that place is itself properly licensed if it is a commercial kitchen.

Step 2: Choose your route

One founder and one truck usually means the individual mobile cart licence. A team, investors or several units usually means a company and a CR.

Step 3: Check the activity requirements

Balady’s free activity requirements inquiry shows the municipal requirements by activity name or ISIC code. Print it and give it to your vehicle builder.

Step 4: Specify and build the vehicle

Brief the builder on the 2025 requirements: interior temperature, condensation-free ventilation, insulation, lighting, non-slip and washable surfaces, potable water, separate produce washing, refrigeration, a first aid kit and space for the required signage and payment terminal.

Step 5: Apply for the licence

Apply on Balady for the mobile cart licence (individuals) or the commercial licence with the mobile cart option (businesses). Pay the fees shown in the platform.

Step 6: Secure your sites

Line up permitted locations before launch: private establishments such as malls, offices and campuses, municipal sites and incubators, and event venues. Get each site permit and agree commercial terms in writing.

Step 7: Health certificates

Anyone who handles food needs a Balady health certificate, which requires a medical examination at a Ministry of Health-approved centre and, where applicable, the health education programme. Allow about ten days.

Step 8: Menu disclosures, payments and launch

Your on-truck menu and any delivery app listings must meet the SFDA disclosure rules in force since July 2025: salt icons for items above 5 g salt, caffeine content for drinks, and calorie-burn activity times. Set up electronic payment, display your unified code, and launch at one strong site before adding more.

Tax and money matters

  • VAT. Registration is mandatory once annual taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 and optional from SAR 187,500. A busy truck can cross the threshold faster than founders expect, so track rolling twelve-month sales every month. See our VAT guide.
  • Separate the money. Even as an individual, run the truck’s takings through a dedicated account and keep receipts for vehicle, stock and site costs. It is the only way to know whether the truck makes money.
  • Freelance certificate. HRSD’s self-employment permit is valid for one year and renewable; the renewal service is free and can run proactively. Keep it active while you trade.

Growing beyond one truck

Most successful truck businesses grow in one of three ways: more trucks at more sites, event and corporate catering, or a fixed outlet once the brand has a following. Each of these usually needs a company with a CR, a licensed production kitchen and proper payroll through Qiwa and GOSI. Plan the switch before you need it. Converting from an individual licence to a company is easier when your books are clean and your brand assets, such as the trade name, social accounts and recipes, are clearly owned. When you are ready for a site, our café and restaurant guides take you through the next step, and the café and restaurant plan model helps you test the numbers.

Getting the economics right

A truck’s low fixed costs make it forgiving, but not unlimited. Before you buy a vehicle, work out four numbers: the cost of the vehicle and its conversion, the monthly cost of keeping it on the road (fuel, maintenance, insurance and site fees), your gross margin per item after ingredients and packaging, and the number of items you need to sell on a typical day to cover everything. That last number is your reality check. If it is higher than the queue you can realistically serve in a trading session, the menu or the price needs to change.

Sites drive revenue more than anything else. A permitted spot at a busy office park on weekdays and an event venue at weekends can outperform a crowded food truck park where twenty units compete for the same customers. Negotiate site agreements that state the fee, hours, power and water arrangements, and how long the arrangement lasts. Keep a simple log of sales per site per day; within a month it will tell you where to be.

A compliant trading day

Inspections happen during trading, so build compliance into the routine. Before leaving base, check that refrigeration is at temperature, prepared components are labelled and dated, potable water is full and the first aid kit is complete. On site, keep the interior within the temperature limit, keep raw and ready-to-eat items apart, and keep allergen and meat-source information visible. At close, record temperatures and sales, discard anything past its holding time, and clean the unit inside and out. A two-minute checklist on a clipboard or phone, signed every day, is the best evidence that you run a safe operation.

Buying the vehicle: new build or conversion

The vehicle is your biggest single outlay, and the decision you will live with longest. A new purpose-built unit costs more but lets you specify every element against the 2025 requirements from the start: air conditioning sized to hold the interior at 25°C or below on a summer afternoon, insulation, condensation-free ventilation, non-slip washable surfaces, a potable water system and space for refrigeration. A converted second-hand vehicle is cheaper upfront, but older conversions were often built to looser standards and may need expensive rework.

Whichever you choose, ask the builder for a written specification that maps each requirement to a feature of the unit, keep equipment invoices and warranties, and photograph the interior and exterior on completion. Plan the layout around your menu: the order in which items are assembled should flow from cold storage to finishing to the service hatch without staff crossing each other. Leave room for the payment terminal, the menu and allergen displays, the meat-source sign and the unified code, which all need to be visible to customers.

Scenarios

A young Saudi founder testing a specialty coffee truck. He uses the individual mobile cart licence, receives his freelance certificate automatically, and signs a site agreement with a university campus. The interior temperature limit drives his choice of air conditioning, and he publishes caffeine content for each drink from day one.

A restaurant group adding a truck for events. Because the group already holds a CR and commercial licences, it licenses the truck through the Balady commercial licence service rather than the individual route, and prepares sauces and components in its licensed central kitchen.

A home cook moving from home sales to a truck. After building a following selling from home, she switches to a truck to reach weekend crowds. She designs a five-item menu, sources ready-made sauces from a licensed producer, and keeps a separate business account so she can see when VAT registration will become mandatory.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a converted vehicle before checking it against the 2025 requirements.
  • Planning to trade in a residential neighbourhood or on an unpermitted roadside spot.
  • Preparing sauces on board.
  • Holding a commercial licence and still applying for the individual mobile cart licence.
  • Taking cash only, when electronic payment must always be available.
  • Letting health certificates or the freelance certificate lapse.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a commercial registration for a food truck in Saudi Arabia?

Not if you are a Saudi individual using Balady’s mobile cart licence, which is issued without a commercial registration. Businesses that already have a CR license carts through the Balady commercial licence service.

Who can get a mobile cart licence?

A Saudi national aged 18 or over who is not a government employee, is not on the municipal suspension list, and does not already hold a mobile cart, retail outlet or commercial licence.

Can a foreigner own a food truck in Saudi Arabia?

The individual mobile cart licence is for Saudi nationals only. A foreign investor would need to register with MISA, set up a business with a CR and license the unit through that business, subject to MISA’s rules on the activity.

Where can food trucks operate?

Only at designated sites such as municipal, government, private-establishment or investment sites and municipal incubators, with a site permit. Trucks may not operate in residential neighbourhoods, near emergency exits or within 10 metres of a fuel station.

Can I prepare sauces inside my food truck?

No. The 2025 municipal requirements for mobile units prohibit preparing sauces inside the unit. Use ready-made sauces or prepare them in a licensed kitchen.

How long does the mobile cart licence take?

Balady quotes one to ten days, and the service needs no documents. Health certificates for food handlers take about ten days, so start them early.

Does a food truck have to accept card payments?

Yes. Under the 2025 municipal requirements for mobile units, electronic payment services must be consistently available, and the unit must prominently display the unified electronic code.

Does my food truck menu need nutrition information?

Yes. The SFDA rules in force since 1 July 2025 apply to food establishments’ menus, physical and online: salt icons for high-salt items, caffeine content for drinks and the activity time needed to burn the calories.
