Methodology

Editorial and AI policy

How we source numbers, how we use AI tools, and how we fix mistakes.

Last updated 6 October 2026

Our number rule

Every number on this site comes from a named, real source, with a link and a date where one applies. We don’t estimate, invent, or round up figures to make a point. We don’t publish made-up statistics, prices, testimonials, or user counts. If we can’t source something, we leave it out and say so.

Sources we use now

  • Exchange rates: European Central Bank euro reference rates, loaded through the Frankfurter API when you open Money Map. Money Map shows the date of the rates it uses. Conversions between two non-euro currencies are calculated through the euro.
  • Currency pegs: for currencies fixed to the US dollar that the ECB does not publish, we use the rate stated by that country’s central bank, with a link to the central bank’s page. If we can’t find an official page, the currency isn’t included.

Reference rates help you understand your position. They are not the rate a bank or transfer service will give you.

Examples

When a page uses example people or amounts, we label them as examples. They are not real customers or real results.

How we use AI

We use AI tools to help write code, draft text, and check our work. AI does not publish anything on its own.

  • A person reviews every page, tool, and chart before it goes live.
  • Every number is checked against its source by a person, not taken from an AI tool’s memory.
  • Calculations run in code that is tested, not in an AI chat.
  • We don’t use AI-generated personas, names, or photos as authors.

Independence

There is no advertising or sponsored content on this site today. If we add affiliate links or sell our own templates, we will label them clearly on the page where they appear.

Corrections

If you find a mistake, tell us and we will check it against the source. When we correct a page, we fix it and note the change on that page.
