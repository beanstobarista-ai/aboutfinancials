MISA investment registration: the foreign investor’s guide

For any business with foreign ownership, the Ministry of Investment is the front door. The updated Investment Law replaced the old MISA licence with a registration system that is faster and simpler on paper, but the documents, activity conditions and annual obligations still decide how smoothly you get through. This guide covers the process from first filing to ongoing compliance.

Information as of 10 October 2026 · Checked against official Saudi sources · About 13 min read

At a glance

  • Who: any foreign investor, including a GCC company with foreign owners anywhere in its structure, must register before investing. Saudi and fully GCC-owned businesses use the Ministry of Commerce instead.
  • How: online through the Invest Saudi portal, with no visit needed.
  • Documents (standard track): the foreign company’s commercial register and last year’s financial statements, both authenticated by the Saudi embassy, plus any activity-specific requirements. Premium Residency holders are exempt from the first three items.
  • Timing: MISA decides within 10 working days of a complete file. The fee is notified on approval and must be paid within 15 business days.
  • Afterwards: issue your CR, get any sector licences, and file an annual update with MISA every year.

From licence to registration: what changed

If you last looked at Saudi Arabia a few years ago, you will remember the MISA licence. That system was replaced when the updated Investment Law came into force and repealed the Foreign Investment Law of 1421H. Licensing gave way to registration. In practice that means a more flexible, declaration-based process, with the burden shifting to ongoing compliance through annual updates.

The law starts from freedom of investment. An investor can invest in any sector or activity open to investment. Local and foreign investors are entitled to equal treatment in similar circumstances. Investments are protected from confiscation without a final court ruling and from expropriation except in the public interest, with fair compensation. Investors can transfer funds into and out of the Kingdom without delay through legal channels, and they can agree to arbitration, mediation or conciliation for their disputes. Those protections are a useful reference point when you are briefing a board or an overseas investment committee.

Two things did not change. Foreign investors must still register before engaging in any investment, and some activities remain excluded or restricted. Investments in securities governed by the Capital Market Law sit outside this registration requirement.

Who needs to register, and who does not

The test is whether the investor is foreign. A foreign investor is any individual or entity that is not a Saudi investor. GCC citizens, and companies fully owned by them, receive the same treatment as Saudi citizens and apply through the Ministry of Commerce, not MISA. The catch is the GCC company with foreign shareholders: MISA treats a GCC company as local only if there are no foreign investors in its ownership structure in its home country.

MISA registers three kinds of applicant: legal entities, foreign companies and individuals who hold Premium Residency. That last group matters for founders who already live in the Kingdom. Premium Residency lets an individual register in their own right, and exempts them from the home-country commercial register and financial statements that the standard track requires.

Whether you also need a Saudi partner depends on the activity. Many activities can be 100% foreign-owned. Others require a minimum Saudi share, and a few need prior approval. Mixed-ownership businesses with a Saudi co-founder still go through MISA, and the Saudi partner’s identity is verified automatically through the National Information Center or Ministry of Commerce records.

There is also a separate registration for non-Saudi companies that want to own real estate in the Kingdom without carrying on any economic activity, which needs certified translations and an appointed representative.

Activity checks and special conditions

Every application is filed against national economic activity codes and runs on one of two tracks. Available activities go through the standard review. Restricted activities, those on the excluded list that are open with approval, need MISA’s prior approval, which MISA refers to the competent authority. Changing ownership in a restricted activity needs approval in advance too.

For fully foreign-owned trading, the obligations go well beyond capital. MISA’s guide lists training 30% of Saudi employees annually, meeting localisation rates set by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development, and a five-year investment commitment, with one option set at no less than SAR 300 million including SAR 30 million of cash capital. Anyone planning a trading entity should read that section of the Investor Guide in full before committing to the structure.

There is no set limit on the number of activities you can register, but MISA expects every activity to be practised once added. Professional activities are registered separately and cannot be combined with non-service activities.

Preparing the file

For regular registration, MISA’s list is short: a copy of the foreign shareholder’s commercial register and its financial statements for the last financial year, both authenticated by the Saudi embassy, a copy of the ID of any GCC individual partner whose details are not in Absher, and whatever the activity requires. The portal shows the exact list for your registration type and activity as you apply.

Three practical points save the most time:

  • Authentication runs per partner. Each foreign partner authenticates its documents through the Saudi mission in its own country, where the service is available. If your partners are in two countries, plan two parallel workstreams.
  • Translation is not required by MISA, though other ministries may ask for translated documents later, so it can be efficient to arrange certified translations in one go.
  • Each company needs its own email address in MISA’s system. Use a role mailbox that will survive staff changes, because official notices, including annual update reminders, go there.

MISA also collects ownership and control information, including those who control the investor or are its ultimate beneficial owners, and it records the establishment’s account manager. Have a clear ownership chart ready.

The process, step by step

  1. Create your account and apply online through the Invest Saudi portal. No visit to the Ministry is needed. The Comprehensive Service Center can guide you, but registration is completed online.
  2. MISA reviews the file. Once all requirements are satisfied, MISA notifies you of the registration within 10 working days. If anything is missing, it sets a deadline of at least 15 working days to provide it. Miss that, and the application is cancelled.
  3. Pay the registration fee. MISA tells you the fee when it approves the application, and you must pay within 15 business days or the registration is void. MISA does not publish a fixed fee schedule in its guide, so budget for it once the amount is confirmed.
  4. Receive the certificate. It is issued in Arabic only. You can have it translated by an accredited translation office.
  5. Move to the Ministry of Commerce. Your contact officer registered with MISA opens the file on the Ministry of Commerce portal. The articles are authenticated and the CR issued electronically. You can also take the MISA registration on its own first and complete the CR later.
  6. Obtain sector licences from the competent authorities. MISA’s Comprehensive Service Center can receive these applications and coordinate with the issuing authority.

If MISA issues a decision you disagree with, you can appeal to the competent court within 30 days of being notified.

Regional headquarters

The RHQ programme is MISA’s route for multinationals that want to run their regional operations from the Kingdom, and it has its own portal. The conditions are specific:

  • The RHQ must be a separate legal entity in the Kingdom, either a company or a registered branch of a foreign company.
  • It may not directly carry out revenue-generating commercial operations beyond its licensed RHQ activities.
  • It must begin its mandatory RHQ activities within six months of the licence, and at least three optional activities, chosen at application, within one year.
  • It must employ at least 15 full-time staff on RHQ activities within a year, including at least three at executive director or vice-president level.

Because an RHQ cannot trade, groups that also want to sell in the Kingdom usually pair it with a separate operating company.

After registration: keeping it valid

Registration creates an ongoing relationship. Most problems arise here, not at the application stage.

The annual update. Every registered investor must file an annual update of the declarations made at registration, including any changes to its recorded data. MISA notifies you 30 working days before the due date and gives you up to 30 working days to submit. You can file within the 60 working days before the deadline, and MISA reports the result within 5 working days. The update fee is notified on approval and payable within 15 business days. The home-country commercial register must still be valid.

Lapses and reactivation. If you miss the update, you can apply to reactivate within three years. After that you must register again from scratch. Operating while the registration is inactive is itself a serious violation.

Changes. Ownership changes, including new or exiting partners, share transfers and inheritance, go through MISA’s ownership amendment service. Afterwards you must amend the partners’ resolution and update the CR within 30 days, or MISA can restore the previous registration data. Adding or removing activities uses the activity amendment service. Merging registrations and cancelling are separate services.

Notifications within 10 working days. Relocating the establishment or changing its account manager without notifying MISA within 10 working days counts as a serious violation.

Questionnaires. MISA can send registered establishments a data questionnaire, which must be completed within 14 working days.

Violations and penalties

The Investment Law distinguishes between non-serious violations, which MISA notifies you to fix within a set period, and serious violations. A serious violation, or failure to fix a non-serious one, can lead to a warning, a fine of up to SAR 300,000 (doubled for repeat violations) or cancellation of the registration. A committee decides the penalty, weighing the gravity and frequency of the violation and the size of the establishment.

MISA’s Investor Guide lists the serious violations. They include investing without registration, investing in an excluded activity without approval or outside its conditions, changing ownership in a restricted activity without approval, giving misleading information, obstructing inspectors, operating without reactivating a lapsed registration, failing to provide requested documents, the two 10-working-day notification failures above, and not meeting the mandatory conditions of your activity.

Scenarios

A European engineering consultancy entering the Kingdom. A fully foreign-owned engineering consulting firm needs presence in four countries and at least 10 years of experience. It registers on the regular track, with its home commercial register and latest financial statements authenticated at the Saudi embassy in its home country, then incorporates an LLC and obtains the professional licence for engineering practice. Because professional activities are registered separately, it keeps any product resale in a different entity.

A foreign founder with incubator backing. A founder whose startup has been accepted by an accredited Saudi incubator applies for entrepreneurial registration with the incubator’s support letter naming the activity number. If the founder already works in the Kingdom, they also submit an employer no-objection letter. The startup then incorporates, often as a simplified joint-stock company, and diaries its first annual update straight away.

A Gulf family company with a minority European shareholder. Because there is a foreign investor in its ownership structure, MISA does not treat the company as local, so it registers with MISA before investing instead of applying directly through the Ministry of Commerce. Later, if the European shareholder exits and the company becomes fully GCC-owned, it uses MISA’s ownership amendment service, which covers conversion to a fully national entity, and updates its CR within 30 days.

Common pitfalls

  • Starting authentication late. It is usually the longest lead-time item, and it runs separately for each foreign partner.
  • Assuming a GCC company is automatically local. Foreign shareholders further up the structure bring it into MISA’s scope.
  • Missing the 15-day payment window after approval, which voids the registration.
  • Treating the annual update as optional. A lapsed registration makes continued operation a serious violation.
  • Changing ownership in the CR first. The MISA amendment comes first, then the CR within 30 days.
  • Relying on old licence-era guides. Requirements such as licence renewals no longer apply.

Frequently asked questions

Is a MISA licence still required in Saudi Arabia?

No. The updated Investment Law replaced licensing with registration. Foreign investors must register with MISA before investing. The registration certificate is issued in Arabic only.

How long does MISA registration take?

MISA notifies the applicant within 10 working days of the file being complete. If information is missing, it gives at least 15 working days to provide it. Most of the real lead time is authenticating home-country documents at Saudi embassies.

How much does MISA registration cost?

MISA confirms the registration fee when it approves the application, and it must be paid within 15 business days or the registration is void. MISA’s Investor Guide does not publish a fixed amount, so we do not quote one.

Do GCC investors need MISA registration?

GCC citizens and companies fully owned by them are treated like Saudi citizens and apply through the Ministry of Commerce. A GCC company with foreign investors in its ownership structure is not treated as local and must register with MISA.

Do my documents need to be translated for MISA?

MISA does not require translation, but other ministries may. Documents must be authenticated by the Saudi mission in each foreign partner’s country.

Can a Premium Residency holder register with MISA as an individual?

Yes. MISA registers individuals who hold Premium Residency, and they are exempt from submitting a home-country commercial register and financial statements.

What happens if I miss the annual update?

You can apply to reactivate within three years of the deadline. After that you must register again. Carrying on investment activity without reactivating is a serious violation that can lead to a fine of up to SAR 300,000 or cancellation.
