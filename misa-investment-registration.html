MISA investment registration: a guide for foreign investors

Under Saudi Arabia’s updated Investment Law, a foreign investor must register with the Ministry of Investment (MISA) before investing. Registration replaced the old MISA licence. This guide covers who must register, the documents MISA asks for, the startup track and what happens next.

Information as of 10 October 2026 · Last reviewed 10 October 2026 · Sources: official Saudi government publications

Short answer

Foreign individuals and companies register with MISA online through the Invest Saudi portal before engaging in any investment. A regular registration needs your home company’s commercial register and last year’s financial statements, both authenticated by the Saudi embassy. MISA estimates 10 working days to process it and tells you the fee on approval. After that, you issue your commercial registration (MISA Investor Guide, Investment Law).

What changed: licence to registration

The updated Investment Law, issued by Royal Decree No. M/19 dated 16/01/1446H (Implementing Regulations), repealed the old Foreign Investment Law. MISA says it “replaced the licensing procedures with registration”. The law covers both local and foreign investors, and it lets them use alternative dispute resolution such as arbitration (MISA). If an article still talks about getting a “MISA licence”, it predates the change.

Who must register

  • Foreign investors must register with MISA before engaging in any investment. Investments in securities under the Capital Market Law are the exception (Investment Law, Article 7).
  • GCC investors count as local, unless there are foreign investors in the GCC company’s ownership. They apply through the Ministry of Commerce (MISA FAQ).
  • Who MISA registers: legal entities, foreign companies and individuals who hold Premium Residency (MISA FAQ).

Registration types

MISA’s Investor Guide lists several registration categories (section 5.1.2). These three matter most to new businesses:

  • Regular registration for activities open to investment.
  • Entrepreneurial registration for innovative or emerging-technology startups that can grow and offer a better or different product in the Saudi market, backed by a support entity that MISA approves.
  • Temporary registration for foreign companies carrying out a government contract. It ends when the contract ends.

Documents for a regular registration

According to MISA’s Investor Guide (section 3.1.1):

  1. A copy of the foreign company’s commercial register, certified by the Saudi embassy.
  2. An ID copy if a partner is a GCC national whose details are not in the Absher system.
  3. The foreign company’s financial statements for the last fiscal year, authenticated by the Saudi embassy.
  4. Any requirements specific to your activity, listed in section 5 of the guide.

Premium Residency holders do not need items 1 to 3. MISA says documents you submit to it do not need translating, though other ministries may ask for translations (MISA FAQ).

The entrepreneurial (startup) track

For entrepreneurial registration, MISA asks for the following (section 5.1.2):

  • A letter of support or proof of project approval from a supervising authority, either a Saudi university or an accredited business incubator. The letter must include the activity number.
  • A board decision, if the applicant is a company.
  • A no-objection letter from your employer, if you are an individual already living in Saudi Arabia.

Step by step

  1. Check your activity. MISA reviews applications on two tracks, available and restricted, and says a review takes up to 10 days (MISA FAQ). Restricted activities need prior approval through MISA’s examination process (Implementing Regulations, Article 16).
  2. Authenticate your documents at the Saudi mission in each partner’s country (MISA FAQ).
  3. Apply online through the Invest Saudi portal. No visit to the ministry is needed.
  4. Pay the fee when MISA notifies you. MISA tells you the amount on approval, and you must pay within 15 business days or the registration is void (section 3.1.1).
  5. Issue your commercial registration and articles of association with the Ministry of Commerce (MISA FAQ). Our CR guide covers this step.

After registration

  • MISA has an annual registration update service (section 3.2.1).
  • MISA issues the registration certificate in Arabic only. You can have it translated by an accredited translation office (MISA FAQ).
  • Serious violations of the registration rules can lead to a warning, a fine of up to SAR 300,000 or cancellation of the registration (Investment Law, Article 11).

Frequently asked questions

Is a MISA license still required?

No. MISA now issues investment registrations. The updated Investment Law replaced the licensing procedures with registration.

How long does MISA registration take?

MISA’s FAQ says the review takes up to 10 days, and its Investor Guide estimates 10 working days for a regular registration.

How much does MISA registration cost?

MISA does not publish a single fee in its Investor Guide. It tells you the amount when it approves your application, and you must pay within 15 business days.

Do I need a Saudi partner?

It depends on the activity. MISA says some activities require a local partner and others do not. Its Investor Guide lists minimum Saudi participation for certain activities.

Can I apply from outside Saudi Arabia?

Yes. MISA says you can apply electronically through its portal without visiting the ministry, after authenticating documents at the Saudi mission in each partner’s country.
