How to open a café in Saudi Arabia

Coffee is one of the most crowded and most rewarding categories in the Kingdom. The paperwork is not the hard part: Balady, the Saudi Business Center and ZATCA are all online. What separates the cafés that open on schedule from the ones that bleed rent for months is sequencing, a site that can actually be licensed, and a menu built for the SFDA disclosure rules from day one.

Information as of 12 October 2026 · Checked against official Saudi sources · About 14 min read

At a glance

  • Who owns it decides the start: Saudi and GCC owners go straight to commercial registration; any foreign ownership means MISA registration first.
  • The core stack: a CR with a café activity, a Balady commercial licence (issued together with the Civil Defense safety permit), health certificates for every food-handling employee, then ZATCA, Qiwa and GOSI.
  • Site first, fit-out second: Balady needs your lease or title deed, a building permit and a shopfront photo, so check that the unit can be licensed for your activity before you sign.
  • Menus carry rules: since 1 July 2025, café menus, printed and digital, must show caffeine content, a salt icon on high-salt items and the activity time needed to burn the calories.
  • Tax: VAT registration is mandatory above SAR 375,000 of annual taxable supplies and optional from SAR 187,500.

Who this guide is for

This guide is written for the founder who wants to open a physical coffee shop: a specialty bar in a Riyadh business district, a drive-through kiosk on a Jeddah corridor, a neighbourhood café with pastries, or a small chain that starts with one flagship. It assumes you will rent a commercial unit, employ staff, and serve drinks and light food to walk-in customers.

If you would rather start smaller, two alternatives are worth weighing before you commit to a lease. A food truck or mobile coffee cart needs no commercial registration for Saudi individuals and tests demand at a fraction of the fixed cost. A home-based business can work for roasted beans, cold brew in bottles or baked goods sold through outlets. Many successful café brands began in exactly that way, then moved into bricks and mortar once the numbers were proven.

If you are planning a full-service kitchen with a large hot menu, read this guide alongside our restaurant guide, because your licensing and fit-out will look more like a restaurant than a coffee bar.

The decisions to make before any paperwork

Format and concept

Your concept drives almost everything that follows: the size and type of unit you need, the activity you register, how much kitchen equipment the municipality will expect to see, and how heavy your staffing will be. A grab-and-go espresso bar with bought-in pastries is a very different licensing and cost profile from a café with an open kitchen serving breakfast all day. Decide which you are building and write it down in one sentence. Every supplier, landlord and official you deal with will ask.

Ownership

If every owner is Saudi, or a GCC national treated as local, you register through the Saudi Business Center like any domestic business. If there is any foreign shareholder, you first register the investment with the Ministry of Investment (MISA). MISA reviews a complete file within about ten days, and only after its registration notice can you issue the commercial registration. Food and beverage is an attractive category for foreign investors, but check the activity on MISA’s lists before you plan around it. Our MISA registration guide covers the tracks, documents and conditions in detail.

Legal form

A single Saudi founder can trade as a sole establishment, which is the quickest set-up but leaves personal assets exposed. Most founders who plan to raise money, bring in a partner or open several branches choose a limited liability company or a simplified joint-stock company. If you have investors on board or expect to, the simplified joint-stock company gives you more flexibility on share classes and transfer restrictions. Our guide to Saudi business structures compares the options side by side.

Budget and runway

We do not publish a cost figure for opening a café, because the honest answer ranges enormously by city, unit size and fit-out standard, and no official source sets one. What we can tell you is where the money goes: rent and deposits, fit-out and engineering, coffee equipment, the first stock order, government and licensing fees, staff costs before opening, and working capital to cover the months before the café breaks even. Build your own numbers bottom-up. Our Saudi café and restaurant plan model gives you a structure to do that.

The authorities involved and how they connect

Founders often feel they are dealing with a dozen separate agencies. In practice the route is joined up, and once you see how the pieces connect the sequence becomes predictable.

Step by step: from idea to opening day

Step 1: Check the activity and its municipal requirements

Before you look at units, open Balady’s inquiry service for commercial activities and municipal requirements. You search by activity name or ISIC code and it shows the municipal requirements for that activity. It is free, immediate and needs no documents. Print the result. It becomes your brief for the landlord, the fit-out contractor and the engineer, and it saves you from falling in love with a unit that can never be licensed for a café.

Step 2: MISA registration, if any owner is foreign

Foreign-owned cafés register the investment with MISA and receive a registration notice before anything else. Saudi and GCC-owned cafés skip this step.

Step 3: Commercial registration

Apply for your CR electronically through the Saudi Business Center, selecting the café activity and any related activities you will genuinely run, such as selling packaged coffee. Under the 2024 Commercial Register Law the registrar decides a complete application within ten days, and one CR can carry several activities. Do not over-register: each activity can bring its own licence obligations. Our commercial registration guide walks through the application and the obligations that follow.

Step 4: Secure a licensable site

Balady will ask for the lease agreement, title deed or investment contract for the unit, a copy of the building permit, an exterior photo that clearly shows the shop sign, and, depending on the contract and activity, a sanitation contract. For activities that qualify for instant licensing, it also asks for a safety equipment invoice or a safety report from the Civil Defense. That list tells you what to verify before signing: the building must have a valid permit, the landlord must be able to provide a registered lease, and the unit must suit your activity. Negotiate a rent-free fit-out period if you can, because licensing and fit-out overlap.

Step 5: Fit-out to the municipal and safety standard

Design the space against the requirements you printed in step 1, not the other way round. Coffee bars often under-plan the back-of-house: hand-wash stations, dish washing, cold storage, waste, ventilation and safe placement of electrical equipment. Your fire safety equipment must be in place and documented because the safety permit rides on the commercial licence.

Step 6: Balady commercial licence and safety permit

In Balady you enter the establishment record, specify the activity and area, pin the location and fill in the shop details. If the activity qualifies for an instant licence you pay the fees and receive it straight away; otherwise the request goes to the municipality for review, which can include an inspection. Balady quotes one to ten days for the service. The fee is shown in the platform before you pay.

Step 7: Health certificates for every food handler

Every worker who handles food needs a Balady health certificate. The worker, or an authorised representative of the establishment, submits the request; Balady checks the business licence and the worker’s profession; the worker completes a medical examination at a Ministry of Health-approved centre and passes the health education programme where the city requires it. Once the examination is complete, the certificate is issued proactively. Balady quotes ten days, so start this as soon as each hire is confirmed, not in opening week.

Step 8: Tax, payroll and hiring set-up

Register with ZATCA, set up your Qiwa establishment file, and register your employees with GOSI. If you are recruiting from abroad, work permits and visas run through Qiwa and the related platforms and take time, so plan your hiring calendar backwards from opening day.

Step 9: Menu, point of sale and soft opening

Finalise your menu against the SFDA disclosure rules described below, configure a point-of-sale system that issues compliant electronic invoices, and run a soft opening. Use the soft opening to time your peak-hour workflow, not just to collect social media photos.

Menu and food safety rules that catch new cafés

From 1 July 2025, three SFDA technical regulations apply to restaurant and café menus, both paper and electronic, and extend to online food ordering platforms:

  • Caffeine content. Beverages must show caffeine content in milligrams per 100 ml or per cup, with a statement that the maximum daily caffeine intake for adults is 400 mg. The SFDA offers a free online caffeine calculator to help.
  • High salt. Items containing more than 5 grams of salt (2,000 mg sodium) must carry a salt icon on every menu and on ordering platforms.
  • Calorie burn. Menus must indicate the time of physical activity needed to burn the calories in an item, using illustrative icons and a duration in minutes or hours.

For a café the caffeine rule is the one that bites. Every espresso-based drink, filter, cold brew and tea needs a figure, and changing your bean, dose or cup size changes the number. Build a simple recipe card for each drink, run it through the calculator, and keep the workings on file. The full technical regulations are available from the SFDA’s Mwasfah store if you want the detailed specifications.

Beyond the menu, treat food safety as an operating system rather than a one-off inspection: temperature logs for fridges and display chillers, date labelling for milk and prepared food, allergen information your staff can actually answer questions about, and cleaning schedules signed off daily.

How long it takes

There is no official end-to-end timeline, and the government steps are rarely the longest part. Indicatively, from the service standards published by each authority:

StagePublished service timeWhat really drives it
MISA registration (foreign owners)Up to about 10 days for a complete fileDocument preparation and attestation abroad
Commercial registrationDecision within 10 days of a complete applicationGetting the activity and trade name right first time
Balady commercial licence1 to 10 daysWhether the site is licensable and whether an inspection is needed
Health certificatesAbout 10 daysBooking medical examinations for each worker
Fit-outNo official figureDesign approvals, contractor capacity and equipment lead times

In practice, fit-out and hiring set the opening date. Run the paperwork in parallel with them rather than in sequence.

Ongoing obligations once you are open

  • CR annual confirmation. Confirm your registered data every year and update any change within 15 days. If the confirmation is 90 days late, the CR is suspended, and every licence issued under it is suspended with it, including your Balady licence.
  • Balady licence and health certificates. Keep both current, and make sure every new hire who touches food has a valid health certificate before their first shift.
  • VAT. Test the threshold every month on a rolling basis if you are not yet registered. Once registered, file returns on time and issue e-invoices through a compliant system. Our VAT registration guide covers the monthly test and filing cycle.
  • Payroll. Pay wages on time, keep GOSI registrations current, and track your Saudization position in Qiwa.
  • Menu updates. Every new drink or recipe change needs its caffeine, salt and calorie-burn information updated on all menus and delivery apps.

Three scenarios

A Saudi founder opening a specialty bar in a business district. She trades as a single-person LLC to protect personal assets, checks the activity requirements in Balady before viewing units, and negotiates a fit-out period into the lease. Commercial registration and the Balady licence are done within weeks; the fit-out and barista hiring take the longest. Her menu team builds caffeine figures for each drink before the menu boards are printed.

A foreign coffee brand entering through a joint venture. MISA registration comes first, with attested parent-company documents. The joint venture is set up as an LLC, then the CR and Balady licence follow for the flagship. Because the brand plans several branches, it designs one licensable unit template and repeats it, which shortens every later opening.

A home roaster moving into a kiosk. After a year selling beans through outlets as a home business, the founder takes a mall kiosk. The kiosk needs a CR, a Balady licence for the unit and health certificates for the two baristas. VAT registration becomes mandatory once rolling twelve-month supplies pass SAR 375,000, so he tracks sales monthly from the first day.

Common mistakes

  • Signing the lease before checking the site. A unit without a valid building permit or a registrable lease cannot be licensed, however good the footfall.
  • Leaving health certificates to the last week. They take about ten days each and depend on medical appointments.
  • Printing menus before the caffeine numbers are done. Reprinting menu boards is an avoidable cost.
  • Registering activities you will not run. Each can bring extra licence obligations.
  • Ignoring the CR annual confirmation. A suspended CR suspends your Balady licence too.
  • Underestimating working capital. Most new cafés take months to reach steady trade. Budget for that period explicitly.

Practical tips from the field

  • Keep one shared folder with the CR, lease, building permit, Balady licence, safety permit and every health certificate. Inspectors and landlords ask for them more often than you would expect.
  • Choose a point-of-sale system that already supports ZATCA e-invoicing and can push menu changes to delivery apps in one step.
  • Make one person accountable for compliance dates: CR confirmation, licence renewals, certificate expiries and VAT returns.
  • Plan the coffee programme with the menu rules in mind. A smaller, well-documented menu is easier to keep compliant than a long one.

Frequently asked questions

What licences do I need to open a café in Saudi Arabia?

A commercial registration with a café activity, a Balady commercial licence for the premises (issued with the Civil Defense safety permit), Balady health certificates for food-handling staff, and registrations with ZATCA, Qiwa and GOSI. Foreign-owned cafés register with MISA first.

Can a foreigner open a coffee shop in Saudi Arabia?

Yes, subject to MISA rules. Any foreign ownership means registering the investment with MISA before the commercial registration. Check the activity on MISA’s lists and any conditions that apply before you plan around it.

How long does it take to license a café?

The published service times are short: a CR decision within 10 days, a Balady licence in 1 to 10 days and health certificates in about 10 days. Fit-out, equipment lead times and hiring usually decide the opening date.

Do cafés have to show caffeine on the menu?

Yes. Since 1 July 2025, SFDA rules require caffeine content in milligrams per 100 ml or per cup on paper and electronic menus and on ordering platforms, plus a statement that adults should not exceed 400 mg a day.

Do I need to register for VAT when I open?

Only when required. Registration is mandatory once annual taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 and optional from SAR 187,500. Track your rolling sales monthly from opening day.

How much does it cost to open a café in Saudi Arabia?

No official source sets a figure, and costs vary widely by city, size and fit-out. Build your own budget from rent, fit-out, equipment, stock, fees, pre-opening wages and working capital. Balady and the Saudi Business Center show government fees before you pay.
