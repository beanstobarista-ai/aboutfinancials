VAT registration in Saudi Arabia: thresholds, timing and compliance

VAT is the tax most new Saudi businesses meet first, and the one that changes day-to-day operations the most: pricing, invoicing software, monthly or quarterly deadlines and record-keeping. Registration itself is free and fully online. The real work is knowing exactly when you cross the line, and being ready to operate as a VAT-registered business the month you do.

Information as of 10 October 2026 · Checked against official Saudi sources · About 12 min read

At a glance

  • Mandatory when your taxable supplies in the Kingdom exceed SAR 375,000 over the past 12 months, or are expected to over the next 12.
  • Voluntary from SAR 187,500 in supplies or expenses. Below that, registration is not required.
  • Deadline: apply within 30 days of the end of the month in which you cross, or expect to cross, the mandatory threshold.
  • How: through the ZATCA portal. The service is free, and you receive a VAT certificate showing your effective date and tax number.
  • Then: charge 15%, issue compliant e-invoices, file returns by the last day of the month after each period, and keep records for at least six years.

Where VAT sits in your tax picture

VAT runs alongside, not instead of, your profit-based tax. Saudi and GCC-owned businesses pay zakat, foreign owners pay corporate income tax, and mixed companies deal with both. Separately, any business making taxable supplies above the threshold must register for VAT. All three are administered by ZATCA, the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, and you will use the same portal for each. Once your business is registered with the Ministry of Commerce, ZATCA generates a tax identification number for it, which is the starting point for every registration.

The standard rate has been 15% since 1 July 2020. Some supplies are zero-rated or exempt under the VAT Implementing Regulations. Which treatment applies is technical, so if your business sells across borders or in a specialised sector, confirm the treatment of your main revenue lines with an adviser before you price.

The thresholds and how to test them

Value of taxable suppliesPosition
Above SAR 375,000 in the past 12 months, or expected in the next 12Registration is mandatory
SAR 187,500 to SAR 375,000 (supplies or expenses)Voluntary registration available
Below SAR 187,500Registration not required

The test is not a once-a-year exercise. The Implementing Regulations require an unregistered resident business to check at the end of every month. It looks back at the supplies made in the Kingdom over the 12 months just ended, and it looks forward at the supplies it expects to make over the next 12. Either test can trigger registration. That forward-looking test matters for a new business that signs a large contract early: you may need to register before you have actually invoiced anything.

A few refinements are worth knowing:

  • Capital assets used in your business, such as equipment you sell when upgrading, are generally excluded from the threshold calculation.
  • Exclusively zero-rated businesses are not required to register even above the threshold, though they can choose to.
  • Related businesses carrying on similar or related activities can be told by ZATCA to combine their supplies for the threshold test. Splitting a business across entities to stay below the line does not work.
  • Non-resident businesses that must pay tax on supplies in the Kingdom must register within 30 days of their first such supply, whatever their turnover. They do this directly or through an approved tax representative.

Timing: when to apply and when registration takes effect

Once you cross the mandatory threshold on the backward-looking test, you must apply within 30 days of the end of that month. Registration takes effect from the start of the month after you apply. Under the forward-looking test, registration takes effect from the start of the first month in which your supplies were expected to exceed the threshold, so you can be VAT-registered for sales you have already made. ZATCA can agree to an earlier or later effective date on request, within limits.

If you do not apply, ZATCA can register you itself, with effect from the date you should have been registered. In that case you may owe VAT on sales you did not charge it on. This is the single most expensive VAT mistake a growing business can make, so treat the monthly threshold check as a finance task with an owner.

Should you register voluntarily?

Voluntary registration is open once your supplies or expenses reach SAR 187,500 over the past 12 months, or are expected to over the next 12. The case for it is usually about input tax. A business in its build-out phase, fitting out premises, buying equipment and paying VAT-registered suppliers, can recover the VAT it pays, subject to the normal rules, only if it is registered. Business-to-business firms also find that corporate customers prefer VAT-registered suppliers.

The case against is the compliance load: e-invoicing, returns and record-keeping from day one. Consumer-facing businesses must also price VAT into what customers pay. Once registered, you stay registered until you meet the deregistration conditions, so make the decision deliberately rather than by default.

How to register with ZATCA

  1. Log in to the ZATCA portal with the business’s account. The CR should already be in place.
  2. Open the VAT registration service and complete the form. At a minimum it asks for the official name, address of the place of business, email, any existing ZATCA identification number, the CR number, the value of annual supplies or expenses, and the effective date you are requesting.
  3. Answer document requests. ZATCA can ask for supporting documents to verify your eligibility, and must give you at least 20 days to provide them.
  4. Receive your certificate. On approval, ZATCA notifies you with a certificate showing the effective date and your tax identification number. The service is free.

After registration, notify ZATCA of any change to the information you provided within 20 days, and display your VAT certificate where the public can see it at your main place of business and at every branch.

Operating as a VAT-registered business

Tax periods and returns

ZATCA’s SME guide sets the default tax period by size: monthly returns for businesses whose taxable supplies exceed SAR 40 million over 12 months, and quarterly returns for those below it. Smaller businesses can apply to move to monthly periods, and after two years on monthly periods can apply to return to quarterly. Returns must be filed, and tax paid, by the last day of the month following the end of each tax period, even when that day is not a working day. Returns are filed through ZATCA’s e-services, and payment is made against a SADAD invoice.

Invoices

Tax invoices must be in Arabic, with any other language shown as a translation, and must include, among other details, the issue date, a unique sequential number, your tax number, the supplier and customer details, a description of what was supplied, the taxable amount, the rate and the VAT in riyals. For supplies to businesses and other entities, and for exports and intra-GCC supplies, the tax invoice must be issued no later than the fifteenth day of the month after the supply. Sales to consumers use a simplified tax invoice with fewer mandatory fields.

E-invoicing (FATOORAH)

Since 4 December 2021, all resident VAT-registered businesses have had to issue invoices through a compliant electronic solution, with additional fields depending on the type of transaction. Phase two, from 1 January 2023, is being rolled out in waves. It requires your invoicing system to be integrated with ZATCA’s systems and to generate invoices in the prescribed format. Check ZATCA’s e-invoicing pages for when your business comes into scope. When you choose accounting or point-of-sale software, check that it supports both phases, because retrofitting is painful.

Records

Keep invoices, books and accounting records for at least six years from the end of the tax period they relate to, and longer for capital assets. Records must be kept in Arabic.

Groups

Two or more resident legal entities under 50% or more common ownership or control can apply to register as a VAT group, with a representative member taking primary responsibility. ZATCA can also treat eligible entities as a group if separate registration produces a tax advantage contrary to the law’s purpose.

Deregistration

You must deregister if you stop carrying on an economic activity, or if a company ceases to exist. A resident business must also deregister when all three of these are true at the end of a month: its supplies or expenses over the past 12 months did not exceed the voluntary threshold, its supplies or expenses over the past 24 months did not exceed the mandatory threshold, and it does not expect to exceed the voluntary threshold over the next 12 months. Apply within 30 days of meeting the conditions. If you do not, ZATCA can deregister you itself.

Scenarios

A Saudi café approaching the threshold. In its first year, the café’s owner runs the 12-month look-back at every month end. In the month its rolling sales pass SAR 375,000, the owner applies within 30 days, upgrades the point-of-sale system to a compliant e-invoicing solution, and reprices the menu to include 15% VAT from the effective date.

A B2B consultancy signing a large first contract. A newly incorporated consultancy signs a 12-month contract that will clearly take its supplies above SAR 375,000. Under the forward-looking test it must apply within 30 days of the end of the month it signed. Its registration takes effect from that month, so its first invoices already carry VAT.

A foreign software company with Saudi customers. A non-resident company that is liable to pay VAT on supplies in the Kingdom registers within 30 days of its first such supply, either directly or through an approved tax representative, whatever its turnover. If it later sets up a Saudi subsidiary, the subsidiary runs its own threshold test.

Common mistakes

  • Testing the threshold once a year instead of at every month end, looking both backwards and forwards.
  • Forgetting the forward-looking test when signing large contracts.
  • Choosing software that is not e-invoicing compliant, or one that cannot integrate with ZATCA for phase two.
  • Not displaying the VAT certificate at every branch.
  • Missing the 20-day window to notify ZATCA of changes, such as a new address or bank details.
  • Relying on penalty figures from old articles. Penalties have been amended over time, so check the current rules on ZATCA’s site rather than secondary sources.

Frequently asked questions

What is the VAT registration threshold in Saudi Arabia?

Registration is mandatory when taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 over 12 months, looking back or forward. Voluntary registration is available from SAR 187,500 in supplies or expenses. Below SAR 187,500, registration is not required.

How long do I have to register once I cross the threshold?

You must apply within 30 days of the end of the month in which you crossed, or expect to cross, the mandatory threshold. If you do not, ZATCA can register you with effect from the date you should have been registered.

Is there a fee for VAT registration?

No. ZATCA’s VAT registration e-services for individuals and businesses are free.

What is the VAT rate in Saudi Arabia?

The standard rate is 15%, in effect since 1 July 2020. Some supplies are zero-rated or exempt under the VAT Implementing Regulations.

How often do I file VAT returns?

ZATCA’s SME guide sets monthly returns for businesses with taxable supplies above SAR 40 million over 12 months, and quarterly returns otherwise. Returns are due, and tax payable, by the last day of the month after each period.

Do I need e-invoicing as a small business?

Yes, if you are VAT-registered. Since 4 December 2021, resident taxpayers have had to issue invoices through a compliant electronic solution. Phase two integration with ZATCA has been rolling out in waves since 1 January 2023.

How long must I keep VAT records?

At least six years from the end of the tax period they relate to, and longer for capital assets. Records must be kept in Arabic.
